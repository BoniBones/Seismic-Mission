## Decisions in the classroom layer

| Claim or feature | Treatment in version 3 | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Magnitude vs intensity | Magnitude describes source size and relates to released energy; intensity concerns shaking/effects at a site. Different published magnitude estimates are not the same thing as different street-level intensities. | Original research; USGS magnitude/intensity FAQ |
| Soft-soil amplification | Use “can amplify certain parts of shaking”; avoid a universal multiplier or fixed ranking of all rock and soil sites. | Original paper’s qualified conclusion; no fixed amplification experiment is claimed |
| Liquefaction | Susceptible saturated soil can lose strength as shaking increases pore-water pressure. The diagram explains a mechanism and keeps grain size constant. Grains do not become water. | Original research; USGS liquefaction definition |
| Loma Prieta | 17 October 1989, moment magnitude 6.9. Qualitative evidence includes damage beyond the immediate source area, Marina ground effects and vulnerable structures such as Cypress. | Original paper; NIST investigation page corroborates date, magnitude and viaduct collapse |
| Christchurch | 22 February 2011. Show liquefaction/ground movement and the role of vulnerable structures; compare the city’s shaking with the larger Darfield event qualitatively. | Original paper; GeoNet / GNS account |
| Christchurch magnitude figures | Do not use unlike magnitude estimates as an exact damage comparison. Source note explains GeoNet heading M6.2 and historical narrative ML6.3. | GeoNet event story as retrieved |
| Approx. 2× shaking, 15,000 homes, 650/90/342 inspected buildings | Retain in the unedited paper, omit from headline visuals and knowledge questions. The supplied bibliography does not establish enough consistent scope to treat them as general scientific rules or damage rates. | Comparison of original draft, source list and v2’s use of these figures |
| Modern buildings | Better seismic design can reduce vulnerability; age alone is not a guarantee. Avoid “earthquake-proof”. | Original paper’s qualifications and Christchurch counterexample |
| Causation | Ground, source/path, shaking and structure interact. The historical cases support the hypothesis but do not isolate every variable. | Documentary method in the original paper |
| Budget and residents | Explicitly fictional. No numerical reduction of risk or lives-saved estimates. | Preserved v1 teaching exercise |
| Laudato Si’ | Residents’ participation and housing/dignity (§§150–152), concern for the poorest (§158), applied to preparedness. It is an ethical connection, not a geological mechanism. | Vatican original text |

## Diagram boundaries

No diagram is a validated simulation. Source rings, wave traces, layer geometry, building dimensions, particle positions, movement and settlement are schematic, without scientific units. They explain mechanisms and deliberately do not predict damage, acceleration, amplification factors, probability or safety. The two lab sites use the same schematic shaking when conditions match. A changed site illustrates a possible mechanism; it does not prove an inevitable outcome.
